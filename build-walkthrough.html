Day 1 · foundations

Nothing visible, everything depends on it

  • One sub-account, Melbourne time, AUD. Four users — Daniel connects his own calendar and Zoom, one-way only.
  • Two subdomains off the zone. Never the root: that is where live mail lives.
  • One Australian number forwarded to Daniel. Privacy and terms published.

Day 2 · assets

Six questions, three of which can end it

  • Eleven custom fields built before the survey, so the builder just drags them in.
  • Radio buttons, never free text, for anything that decides whether someone qualifies.
  • Contact details last, with a reason line. Partial submissions still create a contact, so an abandoned survey leaves Daniel a name.

Days 3–4 · pages

One page, one road, no exits

  • No navigation and no outbound links. The button scrolls to the survey sitting on the same page.
  • No email gate before the video. They watch first, apply second.
  • A clone at /apply-ads so headlines can be tested without touching the original.

Day 5 · automations

The 30 minutes that decide the month

  • W1 waits half an hour. Booked in that window and it stops dead; not booked and the chase starts.
  • W2 confirms and reminds at 24 hours, 2 hours and 15 minutes. W3 catches cancellations and no-shows.
  • W4 is Daniel’s one-question form — it moves the card so nobody does it by hand.

Chapter 7 · before a dollar is spent

Walk every path with a real phone

  • The qualified path end to end, in incognito: card created, Daniel assigned, email and SMS arriving, the event on his calendar, the conversion in Events Manager.
  • Then the ugly ones — disqualified, didn’t book, cancelled, no-show, and replying STOP to an SMS.
  • David walks the whole thing on his own phone. Every video plays, every page fits, every caption reads.

Then delete the test contacts and leave the workflows published.
